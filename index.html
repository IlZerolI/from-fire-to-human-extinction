From Fire to HumanExtinction

Every major innovation moved humanity forward — and every one carries the seed of how we might end. From mastering fire to artificial intelligence, we trace the tools that built civilisation and the risks they now pose.

Explore Innovations Class Groups View QR Code

The Premise

Innovation is what made us dominant. It may also be what undoes us.

Progress

Fire, agriculture, industry, computing — each breakthrough expanded what humans could do.

Risk

Nuclear weapons, climate change, engineered pathogens, misaligned AI — the same ingenuity turned inward.

Choice

The next century decides whether we survive our own inventions or become extinct by them.

27 Students

Innovations — Promise and Peril

Nine breakthroughs, from fire to AI. Each group presents one.

Prehistory
Mastery of Fire
Warmth, protection, cooked food, longer days. The first technology that separated humans from other animals.
Risk today: uncontrolled fire — wildfires, arson, accidental catastrophe.
~10,000 BCE
Agriculture
Settled farming, surplus food, cities, and civilisation. More calories, denser populations.
Risk today: industrial farming, soil collapse, pesticide-driven ecosystem failure.
~1440 CE
Printing Press
Mass literacy, the scientific revolution, and the rapid spread of ideas across continents.
Risk today: information overload, propaganda, and the collapse of shared truth.
1760–1840
Steam Engine
The Industrial Revolution begins. Mechanised production, trains, and the fossil-fuel economy.
Risk today: coal and oil burning — the root of anthropogenic climate change.
1945
Nuclear Fission
The atomic bomb. For the first time, humans could end civilisation in hours.
Risk today: nuclear war, accidental launch, and proliferation of weapons-grade material.
1947
The Transistor
The building block of modern electronics — computers, phones, and the digital age.
Risk today: automated warfare, mass surveillance, and digital infrastructure collapse.
1973
Recombinant DNA
Genetic engineering begins. Editing genes to cure disease and improve crops.
Risk today: engineered pathogens, lab leaks, and bioweapons more deadly than nature.
1989
The World Wide Web
Global, instant communication. All human knowledge connected at the speed of light.
Risk today: cyberwar, AI-generated misinformation, and the erosion of reality.
2020s
Artificial Intelligence
Machines that reason, create, and act autonomously. The most powerful tool yet built.
Risk today: misaligned superintelligence, loss of control, and human obsolescence.
